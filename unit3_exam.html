When we hear about worldwide issues like climate change, poverty, or hunger, it might seem impossible for one person to make a difference. However, these problems can't be ignored—they must require collective action. If everyone takes small but meaningful steps, the combined impact could be huge. We should use the knowledge we gain to reflect on our global responsibilities. For instance, reducing waste, supporting ethical businesses, or advocating for fair policies may seem minor, but together, they must create significant change. Part 3: Written Questions (32-42)

32. Rewrite using a modal verb of deduction: I am sure he is at home because his car is here.

He must be at home because his car is here.

33. Rewrite using a modal verb of deduction: I am sure she didn't steal the money.

She can't have stolen the money.

34. Give advice using "should": I have a terrible headache.

You should see a doctor.

35. Express regret using "should have": I didn't study for the exam and I failed.

I should have studied for the exam.

36. Complete the sentence with a suitable idiom: After his father became ill, he had to .......... of supporting his family.

shoulder the burden

37. What is the main purpose of a persuasive essay?

To convince the reader to take a specific action or adopt a particular opinion.

38. What is a "Call to action" in a persuasive essay?

A sentence that urges the reader to act.

39. Write a sentence to state an opinion about protecting the environment.

I believe that we must take urgent action to protect our environment.

40. Write a rhetorical question about climate change.

How much longer can we ignore the effects of climate change?

41. What is "Evidence" in persuasive writing?

Facts or data supporting your opinion.

42. What is the difference between "Opinion" and "Thesis statement"?

An opinion is a personal belief, while a thesis statement is the central idea or argument of the essay that the writer defends.
