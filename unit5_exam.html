Part 2: Reading & Translation (22-31)

We are now in the year 2045, the world had changed in ways no one expected. Technology had advanced so far that it could do almost everything people once did. Digital systems diagnosed illnesses with perfect accuracy, automated assistants wrote flawless contracts, and machines composed music that moved people to tears. As a result, many jobs disappeared. In response, governments introduced basic income, and schools began teaching "future-proof" skills. However, in a quiet corner of Cairo, a small café remained a place of warmth and humanity. An elderly woman named Amina told stories in a way no machine could match.

Part 3: Written Questions (32-42)

32. Rewrite using the causative verb "make": The teacher forced the students to study.

The teacher made the students study.

33. Rewrite using the passive voice: My father let me go out.

I was allowed to go out (by my father).

34. Rewrite using the causative "have": Someone painted my house.

I had my house painted.

35. Combine using "stop... from": He makes noise. The teacher prevents him.

The teacher stopped him from making noise.

36. What is the main idea of the first reading passage?

While technology can do many things, it cannot replace human emotions, passion, and the special spark in storytelling.

37. What are the main elements of narrative writing?

Characters, Setting, Plot, Point of View, Dialog and Description, Theme, Emotion and Voice.

38. Define "climax" in narrative writing.

The most exciting turning point in the story.

39. What is "foreshadowing"?

Hints about what will happen later in the story.

40. Give an example of a "Common Error" in narrative writing.

Over-description, flat characters, weak endings, or unfocused plots.

41. Write a sentence using the idiom "think outside the box".

We need to think outside the box to solve this problem.

42. Write a sentence using the idiom "climb the career ladder".

He worked hard to climb the career ladder in his company.
